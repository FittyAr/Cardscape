# Security templates

> **TEMPLATES** the deployer (the data
> controller) fills in and uses as
> starting points for their own
> compliance work. The project does
> not publish these on the deployer's
> behalf; the deployer is the data
> controller and is responsible for
> adapting the templates to their
> organisation, running them past
> their legal counsel, and publishing
> / using the final text.

## Contents

- [`privacy-notice.md`](privacy-notice.md) —
  the privacy notice template (Art.
  13/14 GDPR). Publish on the
  deployer's website under their
  own domain.
- [`breach-notification.md`](breach-notification.md) —
  the breach notification template
  (Art. 33/34 GDPR). Use within
  72 hours of detecting a
  reportable breach.
- [`dpia.md`](dpia.md) —
  the Data Protection Impact
  Assessment template (Art. 35 GDPR).
  Required for high-risk processing
  (the AI features, large-scale
  deployment, systematic monitoring,
  new technologies, special-category
  data).
- [`dsar-response.md`](dsar-response.md) —
  the Data Subject Access Request
  response template (Art. 15 GDPR).
  Use within 30 days of receiving a
  DSAR.

## How to use the templates

1. **Copy the template** to your
   own document (do not edit in
   place; the project ships the
   template as a reference).
2. **Fill in the placeholders**
   (`<name>`, `<email>`, etc.) with
   your organisation's details.
3. **Run the document past your
   legal counsel** before publishing
   or sending. The templates are
   the project's recommended
   starting points; your legal
   counsel knows your jurisdiction
   and your organisation's specific
   circumstances.
4. **Publish / send the final
   document** under your own brand,
   not under the Cardscape brand.
5. **Keep a copy in your records**.
   The supervisory authority may
   ask for it during an audit.

## Customising the templates

The templates are deliberately
verbose. The deployer can:

- remove sections that do not apply
  to their deployment (e.g. if the
  deployer does not enable the AI
  features, remove the LLM provider
  section from the privacy notice)
- add sections that are specific to
  their deployment (e.g. a section
  on the deployer's internal
  customer support process)
- re-order the sections to match
  their organisation's privacy
  notice convention

The deployer should not:

- remove the GDPR-mandated sections
  (right of access, right to
  erasure, etc.)
- weaken the security measures
  described in the privacy notice
  (the project ships the security
  measures; the deployer's privacy
  notice should reflect them
  accurately)
- add a section that contradicts
  the project's privacy-by-design
  decisions (e.g. "we share your
  data with advertisers" would
  contradict the project's posture)

## Disclaimer

The templates are provided for
**convenience only**. The project
does not provide legal advice; the
templates are not a substitute for
legal counsel. The deployer is
responsible for their own
compliance with the GDPR and any
local data-protection law.
